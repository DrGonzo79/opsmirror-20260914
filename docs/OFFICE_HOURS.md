# Office Hours — OpsMirror

**Mode:** Startup diagnostic (unattended evidence-based pass)  
**Decision:** Build the narrow refund-audit wedge; reject the generic SOP platform.

## Customer and pain

The first buyer is **Renee, Head of Support at a 30–150 person subscription business processing 500+ refunds monthly**. She owns gross refund leakage, escalation quality, and audit readiness. She is praised for faster resolution without uncontrolled exceptions and blamed when policy drift becomes chargebacks, margin loss, or inconsistent customer treatment.

Status quo: a policy lives in Notion/Confluence, supervisors spot-check tickets, analysts export spreadsheets after an incident, and product changes silently invalidate articles. The desperate use case is the Monday-after-a-policy-change review: “Show me every refund over the approval threshold that shipped without evidence before finance finds it.”

## Narrowest paid wedge

A productized weekly service that accepts one refund SOP plus a Zendesk export, compares 50–500 decisions, and returns a cited exception queue. Initial offer: $1,500 setup + $750/month for one workflow; an analyst verifies mappings before delivery. This can be sold before connectors or generalized agents exist.

## Demand and observation plan

Evidence is directional, not validated demand: teams fail to maintain documents, a single change can touch 8–10 articles, and process comparison has a concrete financial unit. Within 14 days, recruit five support leaders, observe each performing a real weekly refund audit without prompting, then manually deliver two reports. Success: three provide exports, two identify previously unknown exceptions, one pays. Surprise to watch for: leaders may care more about SOP defects than agent non-compliance—the product must allow “policy is wrong” as a first-class disposition.

## Premises

1. Ticket records contain enough structured evidence to make a useful first pass.
2. Support leaders will pay to find exceptions earlier than finance or escalations do.
3. Explainable rules outperform opaque AI scoring for the first wedge.

## Approaches considered

- **A — Analyst-led report (minimal, S / low risk):** CSV + policy mapping + weekly findings. Fast demand test; operationally manual.
- **B — Review workbench (selected, M / medium):** reusable policy rules, evidence queue, verification dispositions. Best balance of paid service and compounding product data.
- **C — Continuous process graph (ideal, XL / high):** live connectors, versioned procedures, event lineage, model-assisted rule extraction. Defensible, but premature without validated mappings.

## Future-fit

In three years, agents will execute more support actions and consume more written context. That increases both the speed of policy drift and the cost of incorrect instructions. OpsMirror becomes the feedback layer between declared process and observed execution—provided it owns verified exception history rather than generic document search.

**Assignment:** Observe one support lead auditing 50 real refund tickets and record every field they consult, especially evidence that exists outside the ticket.
